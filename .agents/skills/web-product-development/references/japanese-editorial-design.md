# Japanese editorial and design criteria

## Before making the page

Establish what the site does, who uses it, and the primary action. Ask a small set of questions when those answers materially change the result. A reference site or mood preference is helpful but not mandatory. Infer a direction when the user delegates taste.

## Japanese copy

- Write for the actual visitor, not for the person evaluating the generated site. Use the visitor's vocabulary and concrete verbs. Keep interface labels short and predictable.
- Avoid interchangeable slogans such as 「未来を切り拓く」「新しい体験を」「あなたの可能性を解き放つ」 unless the specific context genuinely warrants them. Explain the actual benefit rather than stacking abstract praise.
- Keep tone and sentence endings consistent while varying sentence length naturally. Remove repetitive introductions, excessive exclamation marks, gratuitous English headings and translated-sounding phrasing.
- Never invent testimonials, client logos, statistics, awards, biographies or quotations. Verify factual claims and attribution. Label mock content in prototypes and remove unapproved placeholders before publication.
- Read the visible copy as continuous prose after implementation. Remove words that do not help visitors understand or act. Check Japanese punctuation, awkward line breaks and duplicate explanations.

## Art direction and details

- Choose one product-specific visual direction: audience, mood, typographic character, composition and focal point. Make layout decisions beyond swapping colors on a hero/card-grid template.
- Establish reusable tokens for text hierarchy, spacing, palette, borders, radii, shadows and motion. Use effects to support hierarchy or feedback, not as a checklist of decorations.
- Select a Japanese-capable font stack with useful fallbacks. Check actual glyph appearance, weight, line height and wrapping. Do not force letter spacing or manual line breaks that fail on mobile. Additional fonts must have suitable licenses and a loading strategy.
- Give backgrounds, imagery, objects and icons a purpose. Use intentional cropping, consistent icon weights and stable image dimensions. Do not add generic stock images or decoration solely to fill space.
- Inspect optical alignment, text measure, button padding, section rhythm, border contrast and hover/focus/active states. Test long titles, empty content and loading/error states where applicable.
- Make motion interruptible, modest and compatible with reduced-motion settings. Avoid scroll hijacking and effects that obscure content or slow interaction.

## Visual acceptance

Check representative narrow mobile and desktop viewports in an available real browser. Inspect overflow, zoom, touch targets, keyboard focus, contrast and font fallback. Review screenshots for alignment and hierarchy, then fix observed problems. Test the actual primary action, not just loading the page. If browser tooling is unavailable, distinguish source review from visual verification; never claim a screenshot or device check was performed.
